When your family outgrows your current living space, you generally have two options: sell your house and buy a bigger one, or hire **home addition contractors** to build an extension. 

With current mortgage rates making buying a new home expensive, millions of homeowners are choosing to build additions instead. But how much does a home addition actually cost in 2026?

### Average Home Addition Costs

The cost of a home addition varies drastically depending on what you are building:

*   **Sunroom / Enclosed Porch:** The most affordable option, typically ranging from $15,000 to $30,000. These usually don't require extensive foundation work or plumbing.
*   **New Bedroom / Family Room:** Standard ground-level bump-outs cost between $40,000 and $80,000.
*   **New Bathroom:** Because of plumbing, tiling, and fixtures, bathrooms are expensive per square foot. A new bathroom addition typically costs $25,000 to $50,000.
*   **Second Story Addition:** The most complex and expensive option. You have to remove the roof, reinforce the foundation, and build upward. Expect costs between $150,000 and $300,000+.

### How to Hire the Best Home Addition Contractors

Building an addition is effectively building a small house. It requires a foundation, framing, roofing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. You need a highly qualified General Contractor (GC) to manage all these trades.

1.  **Check Licensing and Insurance:** Never hire an unlicensed contractor for structural work. Verify their General Liability insurance and Worker's Compensation.
2.  **Look at Past Additions:** Ask to see photos or visit previous additions they have built. A contractor who is great at kitchen remodels might not be qualified to pour a foundation and tie a new roof into your existing structure.
3.  **Get a Detailed Scope of Work:** The quote should detail exactly what materials are being used (e.g., "Owens Corning Architectural Shingles" not just "Roofing").
4.  **Understand the Permitting Process:** Good home addition contractors will handle all the architectural drawings, engineering stamps, and city permits for you. Do not pull the permits in your own name as the homeowner—this shifts the legal liability onto you!
